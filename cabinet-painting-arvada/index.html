Why brushed cabinets always look brushed

You can tell a brushed cabinet door from across the room. The brush leaves tracks, the paint pools at the panel edges, and the surface never reads as furniture. A sprayed finish lays down flat and even, which is the entire point of painting cabinets rather than replacing them.

So doors and drawer fronts come off, get labeled, and get sprayed in a controlled environment. The boxes stay in your kitchen and get sprayed in place behind containment. The result is a consistent finish on every surface, not a compromise on the parts that are hard to reach.

Four steps, and skipping any one of them means chipping

  1. Degrease. Kitchen cabinets carry years of cooking residue, especially above the stove. No primer bonds through grease. This step is boring and it is non-negotiable.
  2. Scuff sand. Factory finishes are deliberately slick so food wipes off. That same slickness rejects paint. Sanding gives it tooth.
  3. Bonding primer. Not regular primer. A product specifically formulated to grip a cured factory finish.
  4. Cabinet-grade topcoat. Wall paint never cures hard enough to survive fingernails and doors closing a hundred times a week.

Every failed cabinet job we are called to fix skipped at least one of those. Usually two.

Oak grain: the decision you have to make

Arvada has a tremendous amount of golden and honey oak from the 80s and 90s. Oak has deep open grain, and paint does not fill it. You have two honest options:

ApproachResultTrade-off
Leave the grainReads as painted wood, keeps texture and characterNot the smooth slab look from the magazines
Fill the grainSmooth, modern, furniture-like surfaceAdds labor and cost. Worth it if that is the look you want

We will show you both on your own doors before you decide. Neither is wrong.

Refinish or replace, honestly

Refinishing is right when the boxes are solid and the layout works. It is the wrong answer when particleboard has swollen, doors are warped past adjusting, or the kitchen genuinely does not function. If you are in the second group we will say so, even though it means we do not get the job.

What the week looks like

Day one, doors and drawer fronts come off, get labeled and leave. Hardware is bagged and tracked so it goes back where it came from. Days one and two, boxes are degreased, sanded and masked behind containment. Days two and three, primer on everything with sanding between coats. Days three and four, finish coats. Day five, doors rehung, hardware back on, hinges adjusted, walkthrough. Cure time between coats sets the schedule, not labor.

Questions

Cabinet painting questions from Arvada homeowners

How much does cabinet painting cost in Arvada?

Published Front Range data puts professional cabinet refinishing at roughly $2,000 to $8,000 for a typical kitchen. Door and drawer count, whether boxes are included, and whether you want the oak grain filled are the three things that move the number.

Is painting cabinets cheaper than replacing them?

Considerably. New cabinetry in this market commonly starts near $15,000 and runs past $30,000 for custom work. Refinishing is a fraction of that whenever the boxes are structurally sound.

Can you paint honey oak cabinets white?

Yes, it is the most common request we get in Arvada. The question to settle first is whether you want the oak grain visible or filled smooth. We will show you both on your own doors.

Do you spray or brush?

Spray. Doors and drawer fronts are sprayed off-site, boxes are sprayed in place under containment. Brushed cabinets look brushed, and that defeats the purpose.

How long will my kitchen be unusable?

Three to five days typically. You keep the countertops and appliances the whole time. Doors are off for most of it, so plan on open shelving for the week.

Why did my last cabinet paint job chip?

Almost certainly a skipped step: no degreasing, no scuff sanding, no bonding primer, or wall paint used instead of a cabinet-grade coating. We can assess whether yours can be recoated or has to come back to bare.
